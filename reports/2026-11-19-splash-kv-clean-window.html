17× the KV budget, and the memory never moved

A concurrency wave on the --queue-size 64 stack pushed 11.77 M tokens of fresh prefill through Splash, peaking at 2,652,720 prompt tokens = 17.04× the 155,648-token resident KV budget: zero refusals, zero suspensions, zero spill, zero demotions. The reason turned out to be structural, and it is visible in one line of the samples — pages_active pins at 0.39× budget in every round while queued climbs 11 → 47. Extra width becomes queue depth, not KV residency.

2 · The claim this retires an open correction, not a silent rewrite

The 2026-11-16b wave was published as “first KV trip on record: stopped at width 10, 509,370 tokens = 3.89× budget, peak suspended 2, lifetime resource_suspensions +3, demote +1502.” In this clean window, 4.4× that prompt pressure produced no trip at all. So the token-count reading of that trip does not stand: 16b's trip came together with waiting_memory and admission.waiting movement, in a window with operator traffic on the engine, on a pre---queue-size stack.

What is established: prompt volume alone does not trip this stack, at any multiple tried here (up to 17.04×). What stays open: why 16b tripped. Co-tenancy is a candidate description of the regime that produced it, and it has never been reproduced on purpose — this wave does not test that, and the rows are not directly comparable (different flags, different engine instance).

4 · Proving the window, and a self-check that got it wrong the method is the contribution

Tenancy here is a measurement, not a claim: 390 /status samples at 10 s, 7,035 in-flight request records, 7,034 attributed to this wave by the prompt sizes the wave itself recorded (55,265 tok KV, 29 tok ladder). The single foreign request is 98,779 tokens at 01:10:19 — 93 s before the first timed stage, the operator's last message before going idle.

The first version of this check used a size threshold (“over 20,000 prompt tokens is co-tenant”) and printed NOT single-tenant, 6,851 samples. Those were this wave's own long-context prompts. A threshold cannot tell a long-context benchmark from an operator sharing the engine; attribution must use the wave's recorded sizes. Published because a self-check that wrongly says “dirty” is as dangerous as one that says “clean” — it would have thrown away a valid 65-minute wave.
