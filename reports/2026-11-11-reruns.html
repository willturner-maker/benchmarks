2 · Splash engine — 1.6.1 first upgrade diff

Width-ladder rerun under the standing spec (stop only when the aggregate falls toward width-1). The rule never fired again. On M5+ chips 1.6.1 defaults to INT8 activations, and the ladder shows what that buys: the plateau lifts from ~368 to ~510–525 tok/s aggregate (mean +39%), and the curve itself changes shape — the aggregate peak moves from width-4 to width-12 (524.8), with the flat band now w12–32. Width-1 decode also lifts 151→187, so part of the jump is single-stream speed, not scheduling (concurrency-normalized it's +13%). The long-standing width-6 dip persists but gets shallower (0.87× → 0.93× of the w4 peak).

  • KV headline reproduced: 611,244 long-context prefill tokens = 4.4× the 139,264-token resident budget — still zero suspensions and zero spill. The secondary memory signals are instance-dependent as established: this run demoted 645 KV blocks to disk at width-10 (exactly what the 2026-11-06 baseline did there; the 1.6.0 rerun did zero), took one brief waiting_memory hold (59.6 ms), and never touched the full page ring. Prefill walls are near-identical to 1.6.0 (611k tokens in 170.5 s) — the whole gain is decode-side.
  • Open flag: warm time-to-first-token read 294 ms vs 174 ms in the prior rerun (cold ≈ unchanged at 442/430). Single sample under a shared engine with no corroborating measurement — logged, to be settled on the next run.
  • Honest caveat: the probe's single-stream decode lines from this run (81–150 tok/s) were captured while the operator's own session — this very model — shared the engine; they are excluded. The clean-window ladder width-1 row (186.9) is the comparable figure.
  • Not bit-comparable: 1.6.1's INT8 default means numerics differ from the 1.6.0 baselines; differences beyond the established ~2% spread band are real change or quantization, not drift.
